13 areas of everyday work.

Choose an area to see what a practical, reviewable workflow could involve.

01Inbox & email+

Get the next step ready before you open the email.

Sort incoming work, gather the relevant history and prepare the reply or task. You review a clear next step without rebuilding the context for every conversation.

  • Group messages by the work they need: answer, action, waiting or reference.
  • Prepare replies using the thread, approved business information and relevant records.
  • Turn commitments into a reviewable work list and flag replies still needed.
03Documents, quotes & proposals+

Get client-ready work without rebuilding it each time.

Bring approved information, agreed details and templates into quotes, proposals, reports and handover packs. Get a consistent, checked draft with missing details flagged for review.

  • Gather the relevant brief, scope, examples and source information.
  • Produce editable documents in your structure, style and branding.
  • Check completeness, calculations, source facts and layout before final review.
02Calls & enquiries+

Handle routine enquiries without stopping your day.

Answer agreed questions, capture what the customer needs and route the next step to the right person. Give your team a useful summary, with less voicemail and repeated information to work through.

  • Answer routine questions using current, approved service information.
  • Capture enquiry details and prepare or create the agreed follow-up record.
  • Transfer a live call to a person, with context, when it needs their attention.
04Customer onboarding & follow-ups+

Get new clients ready to start.

Bring their information, welcome documents and first tasks together, then show what is still needed. Prepare the next follow-up with the right context so the team can keep the start of the relationship organised.

  • Build a checklist from the agreed service and client information.
  • Prepare the welcome pack, internal briefing and initial task records.
  • Check submitted information and draft requests for missing items or agreed next steps.
06Meetings, tasks & handovers+

Keep work moving between check-ins.

Carry decisions, actions and context into the next agreed step, with clear review points. Your team can pick up the work without recreating the meeting or delivering another full briefing.

  • Extract actual decisions and commitments from available notes, with source references.
  • Keep the working record, output and completion criteria together.
  • Pass work to the next agreed stage and surface missing inputs or decisions.
09Purchasing & suppliers+

Compare the offers on the same terms.

Put costs, inclusions, exclusions and delivery details into a clear, sourced comparison before you commit. See unanswered questions and prepare the next supplier conversation without rebuilding the comparison by hand.

  • Extract comparable information from quotes, proposals and product details.
  • Apply your criteria and show assumptions behind cost calculations.
  • Flag missing information and prepare clarification questions for review.
05Scheduling & coordination+

Cut the back-and-forth around getting people together.

Compare availability, prepare suitable options and coordinate agreed changes in connected calendars. Keep the relevant people, meeting details and preparation together so arrangements are easier to manage.

  • Compare permitted calendar availability with duration, time zone and meeting constraints.
  • Prepare a short set of suitable times and the required scheduling messages.
  • Create or update authorised events and carry the details into the meeting brief.
07Social media & content+

Turn social activity into useful follow-through.

Bring relevant comments and questions into a work list, then prepare replies, customer tasks or content for review. Put what your audience is saying to use without manually rebuilding the context each time.

  • Collect supported comments, mentions and agreed topics that matter to the business.
  • Separate service questions, follow-up needs and useful content ideas.
  • Develop replies, briefs and related content using approved source material and brand guidance.
08Reporting & visibility+

See what needs attention without assembling the report.

Bring agreed figures and status updates together, with changes, gaps and questions clearly shown. Give your team a useful view of the business without the repeated work of collecting and formatting the same information.

  • Gather the selected figures and status records into a consistent reporting structure.
  • Apply agreed calculations and flag unusual changes or missing data.
  • Produce a dated report with source links and a short list of questions to resolve.
10Recruitment & staff onboarding+

Get hiring admin and new-starter preparation organised.

Prepare role documents, coordinate interview details and build induction checklists from your agreed process. Keep the administrative work moving while your team makes the hiring decisions and welcomes new staff.

  • Draft role descriptions and interview materials from approved job requirements.
  • Organise scheduling, documents and administrative checklists for human review.
  • Prepare role-specific induction guides, training material and new-starter tasks.
11Project delivery tracking+

See what is holding delivery up.

Bring milestones, outstanding inputs and blockers into one current view, with the next action ready for review. Give the team a clearer handover and fewer scattered status checks as the project progresses.

  • Reconcile agreed milestones and responsibilities with connected project records.
  • Identify overdue steps, missing inputs and dependencies needing attention.
  • Prepare status updates, handover notes and proposed next actions.
12Industry & competitor monitoring+

Know what changed and what may need a response.

Check agreed sources and prepare a short, sourced briefing tied to your business priorities. See relevant developments, their possible implications and a prepared next step without repeating the research each week.

  • Track accessible industry, competitor and market sources at an agreed frequency.
  • Compare new findings with previous briefings and your stated priorities.
  • Prepare questions, options or follow-up tasks with evidence and uncertainty visible.
13Business knowledge & team assistance+

Help your team handle more of the everyday work.

Make approved procedures and examples easier to find and use, with clear sources and escalation when a manager needs to decide. Give staff practical answers and role-specific guidance while reducing repeated explanations.

  • Prepare answers that point to the relevant approved business source.
  • Turn procedures into guides, checklists and practical learning exercises.
  • Flag unanswered questions and outdated material for the source owner to review.
